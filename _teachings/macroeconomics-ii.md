## Course Overview

This course develops the conceptual and analytical tools needed to construct, solve, and interpret modern microfounded macroeconomic models. It begins with a one-period closed economy, introduces intertemporal decisions in a two-period setting, and then moves toward business-cycle and open-economy applications.

The public materials are currently provided in Spanish, their original classroom language. Website navigation and descriptions are in English.

## Learning Objectives

- Formulate and solve microfounded general-equilibrium models.
- Identify the assumptions and mechanisms behind key theoretical results.
- Interpret macroeconomic data and empirical evidence through the lens of the models studied in class.
- Build a foundation for dynamic macroeconomics, business-cycle analysis, and open-economy models.

## Prerequisites

Students should be comfortable with microeconomics and static constrained optimization, particularly the Lagrange method. Prior coursework in dynamic optimization is not required.

## Primary References

- Stephen D. Williamson, _Macroeconomics_, 6th edition, 2018.
- Pablo Kurlat, _A Course in Modern Macroeconomics_, 2020.
- Stephanie Schmitt-Grohé, Martín Uribe, and Michael Woodford, _International Macroeconomics: A Modern Approach_, 2022.
